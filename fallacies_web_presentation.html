FALLACIES

Why good arguments go wrong

It sounds convincing.

Until you find

the gaps.

A logical fallacy.

On purpose.

By mistake.

Three fallacies.

01 Non sequitur
02 False dilemma
03 Socratic overload
01 / FALLACY

NON SEQUITUR

It does not follow.

a.k.a. irrelevant reason · derailment

NON SEQUITUR — ANIMATED CONVERSATION EXAMPLE
"Lamborghinis are fast."
"So it's a Lamborghini."
PERSON A
"That car is fast."
PERSON B
Step 1: Premise

Lamborghinis are fast.

Step 2: Observation

Lamborghinis are fast.

+

That car is fast.

Step 3: Flawed Conclusion

Lamborghinis are fast.

That car is fast.

THEREFORE:

So it's a Lamborghini.

NON SEQUITUR

Just because the conclusion sounds reasonable doesn't mean the argument actually leads to it.

02 / FALLACY

FALSE DILEMMA

Either…or.

a.k.a. the either-or fallacy

FALSE DILEMMA — ANIMATED CONVERSATION EXAMPLE
"Either you buy this product..."
SELLER
"...or you hate the environment and want the world to end!"
EXTREME CHOICE

What the argument hides:

Or neither.

Or both.

Or more.

Black-and-white thinking.

FALSE DILEMMA

Forcing a choice between two extremes when many other options exist.

03 / FALLACY

SOCRATIC OVERLOAD

The Socratic method.

Questions that test what you really know.

SOCRATIC OVERLOAD — RAPID QUESTION BARRAGE ANIMATION
"What is your evidence?"
"Have you tested every variable?"
"Who defined your terms?"
"What about counter-examples?"
"Can you prove your premise?"
"Why assume logic holds?"
"How do you address paradoxes?"
"Is that universally true?"
"What is the underlying cause?"
"Are you certain of anything?"

SOCRATIC OVERLOAD

Gish Gallop. Too many arguments.

1,000 ≠ 1

bad arguments good argument

Proof by Verbosity.

Too complex to question.

a.k.a. proof by intimidation

Overwhelm.

Intimidate.

Three questions.

Does it follow? NON SEQUITUR
Are there other options? FALSE DILEMMA
One at a time. SOCRATIC OVERLOAD

Question everything.
