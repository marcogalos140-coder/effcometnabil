01 / FALLACY

NON SEQUITUR

It does not follow.

a.k.a. irrelevant reason · derailment

NON SEQUITUR EXAMPLE
"Did you see that sleek red car zoom past the highway?"
"True, Lamborghinis are super fast. And that car is definitely fast..."
"...So that car MUST be a Lamborghini!"
PERSON A
"Yeah, it must be going at least 180 mph! Lamborghinis are famously fast."
"Wait—what are you getting at?"
PERSON B
Step 1: Premise

Lamborghinis are fast.

Step 2: Observation

Lamborghinis are fast.

+

That car is fast.

Step 3: Flawed Conclusion

Lamborghinis are fast.

That car is fast.

THEREFORE:

So it's a Lamborghini.

NON SEQUITUR

Just because the conclusion sounds reasonable doesn't mean the argument actually leads to it.

02 / FALLACY

FALSE DILEMMA

Either…or.

a.k.a. the either-or fallacy

FALSE DILEMMA EXAMPLE
"You're looking at our new Eco-Bottle 3000. Are you ready to buy it today?"
"Look, it's simple: either you buy this Eco-Bottle right now..."
"...or you hate the environment and want the entire planet to end!"
SELLER
"Honestly, it's a bit pricey. I already have a reusable steel bottle at home."
"Or what? I just keep using the bottle I already own?"
BUYER / FRIEND

What the argument hides:

Or neither.

Or both.

Or more.

Black-and-white thinking.

FALSE DILEMMA

Forcing a choice between two extremes when many other options exist.

03 / FALLACY

SOCRATIC OVERLOAD

The Socratic method.

Questions that test what you really know.

SOCRATIC OVERLOAD ANIMATION
"What is your evidence?"
"Have you tested every variable?"
"Who defined your terms?"
"What about counter-examples?"
"Can you prove your premise?"
"Why assume logic holds?"
"How do you address paradoxes?"
"Is that universally true?"
"What is the underlying cause?"
"Are you certain of anything?"
"What if your senses deceive you?"
"Did you cite every source ever written?"
"How can you be 100% objective?"
"Why haven't you answered my first question yet?"

Summary · Group 6

THINK CLEARLY.

Spot the flaw before the argument fools you.

01 · NON SEQUITUR

Check the leap

Make sure the conclusion actually follows from the evidence given.

02 · FALSE DILEMMA

Look for the middle

Reject forced extremes—there are almost always more than two choices.

03 · SOCRATIC OVERLOAD

Answer one at a time

Don't let an endless flood of questions bury the core issue.
